Reference cards are the highest ratio of usefulness to price in nurse gifting. A set costs between $5 and $15, weighs nothing, and answers the questions nurses look up several times a shift for their entire first year of practice.

## What is actually on them

The useful sets cover **normal lab values** (the reason most nurses reach for a card at all), **EKG rhythm recognition**, **vital sign ranges by age**, **unit conversions**, and **drip-rate formulas**. Better sets add ABG interpretation and common medication classes. The point is not that nurses cannot learn this — it is that recalling a potassium range with certainty at 3am on hour eleven is a different task from recalling it in a classroom, and the cards remove any doubt in about two seconds.

## Who they suit best

**Nursing students and new graduates** get the most out of them, and they pair naturally with a badge reel and a badge buddy credential card as a small first-job gift bundle. They are also genuinely useful to **nurses changing specialty** — someone moving from med-surg to cardiac or paediatrics is briefly a beginner again, and a fresh set of cards for the new unit is a thoughtful, unpatronising thing to give. Check whether their badge hangs vertically or horizontally before buying, or pick a set that covers both.
